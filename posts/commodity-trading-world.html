The Commodity Trading World, on Two Axes

Commodity trading is one of the oldest industries on earth and one of the least understood. This is a beginner's map: every actor placed on a grid of what they do (the value chain) against what they move (the commodity class) — so you can see, at a glance, where a firm like Trafigura actually sits.

By Denis Joly · A first orientation piece · July 2026

01The framework

Two axes. The first is the value chain — the journey of a raw material from ground to buyer, plus the support layer that finances and de-risks it. The second is the commodity class. A company is not a point on this map; it is a footprint — the set of cells it occupies.

Axis 1 — Value chain

The operational pipeline, with the merchant at its center
Producers & suppliers — extract / farm / drill
Processing & transformation — smelt, refine, crush, blend
Storage & terminals
Logistics & shipping
Trader / Merchant — orchestrates all of the above
Support & infrastructure layer Trade & structured finance Credit & cargo insurance Inspection & quality control Exchanges & price reporting Audit · legal · risk & fintech

Axis 2 — Commodity class

Three families, each with sub-classes
Energy — crude & products, gas & LNG, power, coal, renewables & carbon
Metals & minerals — base, precious & PGM, ferrous & steel, battery & critical
Agriculture — grains & oilseeds, softs (sugar, coffee, cocoa, cotton), protein & livestock, fertilizers
Why the trader sits at the center: most of the famous names in commodities are not miners or farmers — they are merchants who buy, finance, move, transform and sell. The pipeline exists to serve that function. Two earlier maps split these axes across separate menus and never crossed them; the value here is the crossing.

03Reading the footprint: five archetypes

Almost every firm in commodities is a variation on one of these shapes. The footprint is the fingerprint.

The integrated major · Glencore

Owns the mines and trades the output. Fills nearly the whole grid in metals + energy — production through marketing. Highest capital intensity, deepest moat.

The asset-heavy multi-trader · Trafigura

Trading-led but vertically reaching: terminals, a vast shipping fleet, smelters (Nyrstar), fuel distribution. Spans energy and metals without being a pure miner.

The specialist major · Vitol

One commodity family, done at scale and depth. Energy only — but trading, refining (VARO), terminals (VTTI) and a huge tanker book within it.

The agri-industrial · Cargill

Originates, crushes, processes and ships food. Deep down the agricultural column — from elevators to meat plants — with a financial/energy desk on the side.

The asset-light niche trader · Open Mineral

Pure merchant, one corner of one column (metals concentrates), no hard assets — freight, finance and insurance arranged via partners. The opposite of Glencore.
